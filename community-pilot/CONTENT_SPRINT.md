# Seven-day Nibiru India content sprint

The sprint tests whether practical education can turn passive reach into useful
community actions. Each post has one purpose and one next step. Nothing is
scheduled until its technical details have been checked against current Nibiru
documentation.

## Audience

- EVM developers curious about another execution environment;
- students completing their first public blockchain transaction;
- technical creators who want accurate material to explain; and
- experienced builders who can answer or improve one onboarding question.

## Publishing plan

| Day | Format | Working angle | Desired action | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Short post + diagram | “Where Nibiru EVM fits in a multi-VM chain” | Save the official developer link | Qualified saves and questions |
| 2 | Screen recording | Verify Testnet 2 without connecting a wallet | Run the read-only network check | Completed checks |
| 3 | Debugging carousel | Five facts missing from most “transaction failed” posts | Use the support-report template | Reports created or copied |
| 4 | Community prompt | Share one Web3 onboarding step that confused you | Describe a real blocker | Useful replies grouped by theme |
| 5 | Builder walkthrough | Read a transaction receipt in plain language | Explain one field in a reply | Peer explanations |
| 6 | Contributor spotlight | Recognize the clearest helpful answer from the week | Follow and thank the contributor | Helpful follow-up exchanges |
| 7 | Open retrospective | What people tried, where they stopped, and what changes next | Join the first-build session or propose a fix | Qualified registrations and issues |

## Draft opening lines

Use these as starting points, then rewrite them in the author’s normal voice.

### Network explainer

> Before building anything, I wanted to answer one basic question properly:
> when I say “Nibiru EVM,” which network am I actually connected to—and how can
> I verify it without trusting the interface?

### Debugging post

> “My transaction failed” is understandable, but it gives a maintainer almost
> nothing to work with. A network, timestamp, public hash, receipt state, and
> three reproduction steps can turn the same message into a solvable report.

### Community prompt

> What was the first blockchain error that made you feel completely stuck?
> Share the error, not your wallet details. I’m grouping the answers into a
> beginner-friendly debugging session.

### Retrospective

> This week I tested a small idea: can a troubleshooting tool move people from
> “something broke” to a useful explanation? Here are the attempts, the common
> blockers, and the parts that still need work.

## Quality checklist

Before publishing:

- confirm every network value against an official source;
- distinguish personal interpretation from a Nibiru statement;
- avoid price predictions, token promotion, and investment language;
- include one useful action rather than several calls to action;
- add image descriptions or captions where a visual carries information; and
- record results honestly, including posts that do not perform well.

## What not to optimize

Do not buy engagement, mass-tag team members, recycle the same reply across
channels, or reward people only for volume. A smaller set of relevant questions,
working examples, and returning contributors is the stronger signal.
